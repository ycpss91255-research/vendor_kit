test(just): 21 of 43 recipes are driven by no test, and nothing enforces the bar
## 21 of 43 `just` recipes are not driven by any test

`just` is this repo's only control surface (ADR-00000011), and complete recipe
coverage has been a stated bar. Measured against `just --summary` per module
rather than by grepping, so aliases and `mod?` nesting are handled the way
`just` handles them:

- **43 recipes** across 8 live justfiles
- **22** are driven by something that actually runs them
- **21** are not, and **13 of those have no test of any kind** — not even a
  grep of the recipe body

## The gap is not spread evenly, and that is the finding

Every **consumer-facing** recipe is executed for real: docker / base /
template / skel, **21 of 21**. The uncovered set is almost exactly base's own
self-development surface:

```
root::    default
test::    default, lint, coverage, coverage-path, metrics, system, smoke,
          clean, sync-docs, sync-docs-check, resolve-docs, sync-readme,
          sync-readme-check
release:: default, coverage-badge
watch::   default, pins, value, bump, uncovered
```

No test of any kind: `root::default`, `test::{metrics, clean, sync-docs,
sync-docs-check, resolve-docs, sync-readme, sync-readme-check}`,
`watch::{default, pins, value, bump, uncovered}`.

The whole `watch` namespace — five recipes, added 2026-09-04 with #987 — has
nothing. And the scheduled workflow that is meant to make "what CI runs is what
a maintainer runs by hand" true **bypasses the recipes and calls the scripts
directly**, so even that is not incidental coverage.

## Two recipes that look covered and are not

- `justfile_user_spec.bats:157` names `prune` and `setup` in its title and does
  not run them; the stubs at lines 105-114 sit unused. Both are saved only by
  the CI acceptance job — a different gate on a different trigger — so
  `just test` alone would not catch a break in either.
- `release::coverage-badge`'s only assertions are greps for two doc-comment
  literals, not for what the recipe does. It writes a **committed artifact**
  (`doc/badge/coverage.svg`) that rides a release commit.

## Why the distinction matters

A spec that tests the underlying shell function is not a test of the recipe.
The recipe is the surface a user touches, and its argument plumbing, its `mod?`
wiring and its default are where it breaks — none of which the function sees.
That is why the count above is strict about what qualifies.

Not every uncovered recipe is a defect: a pure delegation with nothing of its
own is defensible. A recipe with argument handling or a side effect is not, and
`test::coverage-path` (takes a spec path), `test::sync-docs` (writes generated
files), `watch::bump` (takes a name and a version) and
`release::coverage-badge` (writes a committed artifact) are all in the second
class.

## The part worth fixing first

**Nothing enforces this.** The bar was stated and there is no mechanism holding
it, which is why a namespace could be added yesterday with five recipes and no
tests and nothing objected. A count that must be re-audited by hand decays
between audits by construction.

So the first slice is the guard, not the tests: enumerate the recipes from
`just --summary` (derived, not a roster) and fail when one has no spec that
invokes it. Then the uncovered set is a list that shrinks under CI rather than
a finding someone has to go looking for. The exemption for pure delegations
must be declared per recipe with a reason, in the file, so that the exemption
itself is reviewable — the pattern the pin-coverage lint uses for `unpinned`.

## Not measured

Whether the 166 existing specs currently pass — this audit was read-only and
counted what the tests assert, not their result.

Found by an audit prompted by cutting v0.43.0-rc1. Refs ADR-00000011, refs #987.
