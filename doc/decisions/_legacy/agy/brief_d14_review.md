## Context

Recorded by the surface audit as `doc/spec/surface.md` §9, S-4, as an
observation. It is now a live one, because the surface has started growing:
ADR-00000013 added four flags, ADR-00000017 added two frozen files, and
ADR-00000020 added a versioned flag value — none of which says whether any
of it may later be taken away.

## Problem

Everything the engine ships is versioned except the thing a caller types.

`doc/spec/surface.md` §9, S-4, the finding as it stood: "**Whether the
surface itself is versioned.** The manifest format has `manifest_version`,
the handoff has a protocol version, the shipped tree has `.version` — argv
has nothing. Nothing says whether a flag or a verb may be removed in a minor
engine release, which matters because a downstream repo's `just` recipe and
a CI job both hardcode it, and because Invariant 4 freezes paths without
saying anything about what a frozen path accepts on its command line.
*Settled by:* a compatibility promise, or an explicit statement that there is
none."

**ADR-00000013 added four flags to a surface with no compatibility promise,
and said so in its own consequences.** ADR-00000013 §Consequences: "**Nothing
here is versioned as a surface.** Whether a flag or a verb may be withdrawn
in a minor engine release is `doc/spec/surface.md` S-4 and remains open;
this decision adds flags to that question's scope without answering it."

The four are `-q`/`--quiet`/`--silent`, `-v`/`--verbose`, `--json` on
`verify`, and `--porcelain` on `upgrade --dry-run` and `outdated`. Two of
them land on the frozen surface: ADR-00000013 §Consequences, "**Two flags
are added to the frozen surface** — `--porcelain` on `upgrade --dry-run`,
and `-q`/`-v` on everything."

**ADR-00000020 versioned the format and left argv where it was.** S-4 is now
marked "**Narrowed by ADR-00000020, and still open for argv**", and the
narrowing is exact: `--porcelain=v1` is a promise about what the *output*
looks like, not about whether the *flag* survives. The same ADR makes an
unrecognised version "a usage error rather than a silent fall back", which
is a compatibility rule for one flag's values and for nothing else.

**One of the four is already recorded as provisional.** #11 records the
`--json` / `--porcelain` split as "chosen under a constraint" rather than
"settled forever", and names a resolution condition under which "the two
forms can become one". Converging two forms into one is a withdrawal of a
flag from a published surface. Whether that is permitted, and at what
version step, is this question — and #11 currently assumes an answer it does
not have.

**A frozen path says nothing about what it accepts.** `doc/PRD.md`
Invariant 4 promises paths: "Released downstream repos hardcode the entry
points they call. Those paths stay valid forever." A path that stays valid
while the flag a CI job passes it stops being accepted is a promise kept in
the letter only. Since ADR-00000017 there are four such paths rather than
two, each "frozen by Invariant 4 from the release that introduces it". The
engine's own vocabulary makes the asymmetry visible: the manifest reader's
rule is stated exactly, `doc/spec/manifest.md` §3 — a reader "reads every
document whose major matches its own, and only a differing major is refused"
— and argv has no equivalent sentence anywhere.

**The removals already in the corpus were free, and the next one may not
be.** `--check` was removed by ADR-00000009 before anything shipped
("the word `--check` is not used anywhere"), and exit code `10` was retired
with the care a *published* surface needs — `doc/spec/lifecycle.md` §5.1,
"`10` is retired" and "Nothing reuses the number, so an operator who meets a
`10` from an old vendored copy is not misled by a new meaning." That care
was spent on a number, and ADR-00000020 spent it again by numbering the next
code `11` rather than reusing `10`. Nothing states the equivalent rule for a
flag or a verb.

**The stability wording that does exist covers a format, not the surface.**
ADR-00000013 gives `verify --json` a promise borrowed from git — "stable
across engine versions **and regardless of user configuration**" — and
versions it with a `schema` field; ADR-00000020 gives `--porcelain` a
version in the flag value. Both are statements about the bytes that come
out. Neither says anything about whether the *flag* survives.

## Proposal

**The deliverable is a decision, and the honest answer may be "there is
none"** — which is still an answer, and one an agent cannot choose on the
maintainer's behalf. What a resolution has to settle:

- Whether there is a compatibility promise for argv at all, or an explicit
  statement that there is none. Both are answers; the absence is not.
- If there is one, what it is scoped to: verbs, flags, flag *synonyms*
  (`-q`/`--quiet`/`--silent` are three spellings of one option under
  ADR-00000013), positional arguments, flag *values* (`--porcelain=v1`), and
  the machine-readable field sets behind `--json` and `--porcelain`.
- What "a minor engine release" means for the engine, given the engine has
  no version of its own that a repo can report — #5.
- Whether a withdrawal is announced, deprecated, or simply gone; and whether
  an unknown flag's exit `1` (`doc/spec/lifecycle.md` §5.1, code 1, "Usage
  error — unknown flag, missing argument") is considered an acceptable way
  for a caller to learn a flag was withdrawn.
- Whether the promise differs across the seam ADR-00000008 draws. The frozen
  surface's flags cannot be fixed for a release — `doc/PRD.md` Invariant 5,
  "Whatever runs from that copy cannot be fixed for a release" — so a flag
  withdrawn there is withdrawn from a copy that is still running in the
  field.
- Whether it governs the four frozen entry-point *files* as well as what
  they accept, since ADR-00000017 makes "Adding a verb now costs a frozen
  path" and says nothing about removing one.
- Where the promise is recorded so a reader finds it: `doc/PRD.md` as an
  invariant beside Invariant 4, or `doc/spec/surface.md` as a table-wide
  property beside "a machine-readable flag never changes an exit code".
- Whether it is assertable. `doc/spec/testing.md` holds a level for
  everything else; a compatibility promise over argv is either a review rule
  or a spec that runs an old caller's command line against a new engine.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] `doc/spec/surface.md` §9, S-4 carries a closure pointer rather than
      "still open for argv".
- [ ] The corpus states, in one place a reader will find, either the
      compatibility promise for argv or that there is none.
- [ ] The statement says what it covers — verbs, flags, synonyms,
      positionals, flag values, field sets — rather than leaving a class of
      the surface unaddressed.
- [ ] It says what a withdrawal looks like from the caller's side: announced,
      deprecated, or an exit `1` on the next run.
- [ ] It is placed beside `doc/PRD.md` Invariant 4 or in
      `doc/spec/surface.md` as a table-wide property, so a reader who found
      the path promise finds this one.
- [ ] It either names a level from `doc/spec/testing.md` that asserts it, or
      records that it is held by review.
- [ ] #11's resolution condition — "the two forms can become one" — is
      either permitted by the answer or is recorded as a move this repo
      cannot make.

## Out of scope

- **Which flags exist.** Settled by ADR-00000013 and recorded in
  `doc/spec/surface.md` §2.
- **Which frozen entry-point files exist.** Settled by ADR-00000017.
- **Whether the two machine-readable forms converge.** #11, which this
  question is a precondition for rather than a duplicate of.
- **Exit-code stability**, which is separately promised: `doc/spec/lifecycle.md`
  §5.3, rule 4, "**100 means one thing on every surface and is never assigned
  twice**", and ADR-00000013's "**Neither form changes an exit code**".
- **Entry-point paths**, frozen by `doc/PRD.md` Invariant 4. This question is
  about what those paths accept, not where they are.
- **The porcelain format's own versioning**, settled by ADR-00000020. This
  question is about the flag, not the bytes.

**Related:** #11 (the provisional split, whose convergence would be a
withdrawal), #12 (the residue of the argv question this promise would first
govern), #5 (no reportable engine release, so "a minor release" has nothing
to be read from), #4 (the codes a withdrawn flag's usage error would land
on).
